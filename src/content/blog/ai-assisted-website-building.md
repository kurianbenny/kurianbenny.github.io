AI tools can help turn a brief into a first draft: a page outline, a layout, initial copy, or a piece of application code. That makes it easier to explore an idea and see something concrete before committing to a design.

The useful question is how to turn that draft into a website you understand and can maintain.

## Begin with the business, then describe the page

Before asking for a design, write down who the website serves, what those visitors need, and what they should do next.

For a local service business, that might be: “Help visitors understand the service, see where it is offered, and send an enquiry.” This gives you a way to judge the result beyond whether the homepage looks attractive.

## Use AI for drafts you can review

A starting layout or a proposed FAQ is easier to evaluate than a blank page. Ask for a small piece at a time, then check it against your real information.

Review names, contact details, descriptions, and claims. Remove invented testimonials or achievements. If a draft says your business offers something, make sure it really does.

For content you will update regularly, keep it separate from layout code. Markdown is one practical option for articles, service descriptions, and FAQs.

## Test the work that happens after a click

A button can look finished while doing nothing. A contact form can display a confirmation without delivering an email.

Follow the main visitor journey yourself: open a service, send a test enquiry to your own configured inbox, and check the result. Try the same journey on a phone and with a keyboard.

For a booking or payment flow, write down what should happen when a step fails as well as when it succeeds. That makes gaps easier to discuss with a developer.

## Choose hosting around the website’s behavior

A portfolio or brochure site may work as generated static pages. A product with accounts, private records, or custom server logic needs additional infrastructure.

[GitHub Pages publishes static websites](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/what-is-github-pages). A form on such a website needs a separate submission service or backend. Putting a form on a page does not create email delivery by itself.

## Know when to bring in professional help

Ask for a review when you cannot explain how the site stores data, checks access, handles secrets, or recovers from a failed deployment. Those are useful signs that the next step needs more than another visual revision.

Professional help can also make an AI-generated codebase easier to maintain: clarify its structure, remove unnecessary complexity, and document how to publish changes.

**A practical handoff:** bring the draft, the code, your visitor journey, and a list of questions. A developer can work from that concrete starting point.

A website needs to explain what you do, help visitors take the next step, and make its important pages easy to discover. Improving SEO starts with those everyday jobs.

For a small service business, begin with the page that describes your main service. Someone arriving there should understand who it is for, what problem it addresses, and how to contact you.

## Make every page answer a real question

A service page might answer “Can you help improve an existing application?” A guide might answer “What should I check before launching my website?” Give each page a clear purpose and write for the person asking that question.

A useful exercise is to ask someone unfamiliar with your business to read the page. Can they explain your offer back to you? Where do they hesitate? Those gaps are good places to improve the copy.

## Check how your pages are discovered

Use descriptive URLs and meaningful internal links. Link your service overview to each service page, and connect relevant articles to those services. Include important URLs in a sitemap.

Google’s [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) explains how useful content, clear organization, and links help people and search engines understand a website. A sitemap supports discovery; it does not guarantee indexing or rankings.

## Make the search preview understandable

Write a title that identifies the page’s subject and a description that summarizes its value. Avoid titles that are just a business name repeated across every page.

For example, “PHP application development — your business name” tells a visitor more than “Services.” The description can explain the kind of work offered without packing in a list of unrelated keywords.

## Test the visitor’s next step

On a phone, try reading the service page and sending an enquiry. Check that text is readable, buttons are easy to use, and the form actually reaches the intended inbox.

If your page includes large images, compare how it feels on an ordinary mobile connection. A beautiful layout loses its usefulness when visitors struggle to load it or find the contact button.

## Keep SEO connected to the business

Decide what a useful visit means: an enquiry, a booking, or a reader finding an answer. After launch, review which pages are being discovered and which questions people still ask.

Treat that information as an editing queue. Improve an unclear explanation, add an answer to a recurring question, or make a relevant service easier to reach.

## What about AI search visibility?

AI-generated search answers do not remove the need for these foundations. Google says its [AI search features use the same foundational SEO practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), including accessible content and structured data that reflects what visitors can see. Special AI markup is not a guarantee of inclusion.

**A sensible first task:** improve one important service page, link it clearly, and test the contact journey. Build from there.
